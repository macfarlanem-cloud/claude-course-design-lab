# AGRI 110: Introduction to Sustainable Agriculture

Sample syllabus for the lab. The course, the college, and the details are invented so you can experiment freely. Replace this file with one of your own syllabi whenever you are ready.

## Course description

A survey of the principles and practices of sustainable agriculture: soil health, water management, crop and livestock systems, integrated pest management, farm economics, and the social and cultural dimensions of food production. Emphasis on the Sacramento Valley and Sierra foothills region. 3 units. Fully online, asynchronous.

## Course learning outcomes

By the end of this course, learners will be able to:

1. Explain the ecological principles that underlie sustainable farming systems.
2. Evaluate a farm practice for its effects on soil, water, biodiversity, and profitability.
3. Compare conventional and sustainable approaches to a specific production problem using evidence.
4. Communicate an agricultural recommendation to a non-specialist audience.

## Week 6 in more detail (the week the lab redesigns)

Current version: learners read the textbook chapter on cover crops and rotation, watch a 40-minute recorded lecture, and submit a two-page case study comparing two cover crop options for a hypothetical 20-acre almond orchard. There is no discussion this week. The case study is graded on a 20-point scale with no rubric posted.

## Grading

Discussions 20%, weekly work 30%, midterm 20%, final project 30%.

## Course policies

Late work accepted up to one week with a 10% reduction. Communicate early if life gets in the way; flexibility is easier to arrange before a deadline than after. All course videos are captioned. Contact the instructor through Canvas Inbox; replies within one business day.
